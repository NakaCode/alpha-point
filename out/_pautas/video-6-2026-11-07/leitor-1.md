# Leitor 1 — Mia, 34, Brisbane

Cold read, first impressions only. Primary teacher, geography and history explainers on the bus, phone in hand. I skip travel vlogs and clickbait lists.

## Stage A — titles alone

**1. "Why the World's Longest Beach Is Almost a Straight Line"**
1. I'd expect a map-and-satellite explainer: a very long beach seen from above, then something about waves, sand and coastlines. I'd half-expect a drawn diagram of the shoreline.
2. Travel vlog? No. "Why" plus "straight line" says explainer, and nobody in a vlog says "straight line". Nothing here says "I visited".
3. Click: **7/10.** "Almost" makes it sound like a real observation, not a hype claim. I'm curious why a beach would be straight at all, since I'd picture beaches as wiggly.

**2. "Why the World's Longest Beach Is So Straight"**
1. Same video as the first, I think: a coastline and the reason it runs so straight.
2. Travel vlog? No, but it's blander and could be a generic "cool facts" video.
3. Click: **6/10.** "So straight" is flat. It tells me nothing, and "almost" in title 1 made me lean in because it hints at a catch.

**3. "How Long Is the World's Longest Beach, Really?"**
1. A measuring video. I'd expect a couple of competing numbers, maybe a "the official claim is wrong" reveal, and probably the coastline paradox.
2. Travel vlog? Not quite, but it's close to a trivia or "top facts" video, and it could have someone walking the beach.
3. Click: **5/10.** "Really?" is the clickbait tell for me. It's a clear question, but I've seen this shape a hundred times.

My pick of the three: title 1.

## Stage B — the map

**4. Does "Almost a Straight Line" feel honest, a stretch, or misleading?**
**Honest, but only just.** With the numbers (about 136 miles of beach against about 132 miles of straight line, so only about 4 miles more), "almost" is fair. If I only had the picture, I'd say it's a stretch. The blue line clearly wobbles across the red dashed one, and that 11-mile bow in the middle reads as a real curve. Because the map is zoomed to fill the frame, it looks bendier than a 3% difference sounds. I wouldn't call it misleading, but the video has to put the 136 against 132 on screen early. If it hides them, I'd feel cheated. A claim in the title that the video then deflates would lose me.

**5. My natural word for the shape:** a **gentle S-curve**, or a lazy S. I'd say "a long shallow S", or "it wiggles a bit around the straight line".

## Stage C — thumbnails, with title 1

**6. "158 MILES?"** (magnifying glass and ruler on the sand)
I read it as "the real length is disputed". But it clashes with title 1. After the map I know 136, so 158 makes me ask where it comes from. If it were really 158 against 132, it wouldn't be "almost straight". It sells a different video (the "how long, really?" one). Click: **6/10**, and a little confusing. I also think in kilometres, so I'd have to convert, though a US-audience channel will want miles.

**7. "WHO DREW THIS?"** (beach with a thin straight line beside it)
**This is the one I'd click.** A natural beach next to a ruler-straight line looks like a borderline mystery, and it matches the "straight line" in the title. It's a bit confusing at first: who drew what? Am I looking at a border or a map error? But that confusion is the hook. Mild risk: it could read as a conspiracy video. If the answer is "waves did", I'd be fine as long as the video pays it off. Click: **8/10.**

**8. "SAME LENGTH?"** (thick ruler and thin ruler over the beach)
**This confuses me most.** Same as what? Two rulers of different thickness over one beach looks like an optical illusion, not a geography video. The thick and thin ruler idea is clever once I know the story, but cold it's vague. Click: **5/10.**

Order: WHO DREW THIS? > 158 MILES? > SAME LENGTH?
