## 5. Para os leitores

This video is about the deepest point in the Atlantic Ocean: a spot more than five miles down (about 27,500 feet), in an undersea trench about 83 miles
off the coast of Puerto Rico, a U.S. island. It opens with two comparisons: if Mount Everest stood on that spot, its top 1,500 feet or so would stick
out of the water, and the Mariana Trench, the place most people think of as the deepest ocean, is in the Pacific, about 9,500 miles away. From there
it follows how people measured and explored the trench, from a weighted wire lowered in 1883 and a Navy cruiser's sonar reading in 1939 to the first
person to reach the deepest point, an explorer who went down alone in his own submersible in December 2018. Along the way it covers why the trench is so
deep, the old landslides and tsunamis around it, a rival trench in the far South Atlantic that turned out about 370 feet shallower, and a 2019 study
showing that the official list of undersea features still had the Atlantic more than 1,000 feet too deep. One thread runs from the start to the end: in
1964 the crew of a French deep-diving craft reported counting about 200 fish some 24,000 feet down in the trench; in 2019 a submersible went back to the
same spot, saw no fish of that kind but found sea cucumbers spread about as thickly and similar in look, and a 2020 paper concluded that the 1964 "fish"
were sea cucumbers, a conclusion drawn from the count and the look, since the animals seen that day can't be checked again.
